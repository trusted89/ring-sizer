Ring Sizer prototype

Measure a ring's inner diameter from one photo

Lay the ring next to a printed 20 mm marker, take a photo from above, upload it. You get the diameter, an honest uncertainty, and the ring size, or a clear request to retake.

1 Print the marker sheet

Print at 100% / "Actual size", never "Fit to page". Then check the 100 mm line on the sheet with a ruler.

Download A4 sheet (PDF) US Letter (PDF)

2 Take the photo

  1. Sheet on a flat table, in soft even light. No flash, no hard shadows.
  2. One ring, lying flat inside the corner marks, with white paper showing through it.
  3. Phone flat and directly above the ring, about 30 cm away. No zoom.
  4. Marker and ring both fully in frame. Tap to focus, hold still.

3 Upload it

What this can and cannot do

Supported conditions
  • One ring and the printed 20 mm marker on the same flat, white sheet, both fully visible.
  • Near-overhead photo: camera within about 10° of straight down. Steeper photos are rejected.
  • Sharp focus: edge blur on the marker under 0.65 mm, and at least 5 pixels per millimetre.
  • The ring's opening must show paper, with a visible inner edge around at least half of the circle.
  • Plain round bands. Stones, settings or bent rings that stop the ring lying flat are not supported.
How the measurement works
  1. Scale. The marker's four edges are located to a fraction of a pixel. Its known 20 mm side gives millimetres per pixel and the tilt of the paper.
  2. Flatten. The photo is mathematically flattened to a straight-down view of the paper using the marker.
  3. Inner edge. 240 rays from the centre of the opening find the paper-to-ring edge. The largest set of edge points that agree on one circle is the inner boundary.
  4. Checks. Missing marker, blur, steep angle, low resolution or an unclear edge stop the measurement and ask for a retake.
Measurement uncertainty

The ± value is a model, not a guarantee. It combines pixel resolution, edge blur, scatter of the inner edge, marker localisation, residual perspective, the distance between marker and ring, and the ring's height.

The ring's height matters most: the upper rim sits closer to the lens than the paper, so it looks slightly larger and can hide part of the opening. The model assumes a 4 mm tall band and never reports better than ±0.2 mm. Wide bands (over about 5 mm) photographed from close up can read high by more than the stated value.

Accuracy has only been checked on the test set listed in the repository. Treat the result as an estimate to within about one ring size, not a jeweller's measurement.

Time and cost per image

All computation runs in your browser, so there is no per-image variable cost: no recognition API, no model inference fee, no paid intermediary, and retakes cost nothing. The processing time shown with each result is measured on your device.

Hosting is separate: static files on Cloudflare. The one-off download of the measurement engine is about 13 MB uncompressed and is cached by the browser afterwards.

Prototype. Uses OpenCV.js (Apache-2.0) for image operations; measurement logic, checks and interface are original work.
